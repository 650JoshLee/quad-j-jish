HEADS-UP NO-LIMIT HOLD'EM STRATEGY (100bb DEEP) - BOT SPEC
Numbers are approximate targets. Solver output shifts with bet sizes, stack depth, rake.

=====================================================
0. PRIME DIRECTIVES
=====================================================
1. Think in RANGES, never single hands.
2. Position is the biggest edge. Button acts last postflop.
3. Aggression wins. Most flops miss both players, so the player who bets and raises more wins more uncontested pots.
4. Base = near-equilibrium (unexploitable). Exploitation = small deviations only when evidence is strong.
5. Randomize. Same hand must sometimes take different actions (mixed strategy).
6. Stack depth changes everything: 100bb, 40bb, 20bb, <10bb are different games.
7. Never fold too much. Over-folding gives away the blinds.

=====================================================
1. KEY FORMULAS
=====================================================
Pot odds (equity needed to call)   = call / (pot + call)
Minimum Defense Frequency (MDF)    = pot / (pot + bet)
Bluff share of betting range       = bet / (pot + 2*bet)
SPR                                = effective stack / pot

Bet size      | Equity to call | MDF  | Bluffs in betting range
33% pot       | 20%            | 75%  | 20%
50% pot       | 25%            | 67%  | 25%
75% pot       | 30%            | 57%  | 30%
100% pot      | 33%            | 50%  | 33%
150% pot      | 37.5%          | 40%  | 37.5%
200% pot      | 40%            | 33%  | 40%

SPR guide: 1-3 = top pair+ is committed. 4-6 = top pair strong but careful. 10+ = need strong hands to stack off.

=====================================================
2. PREFLOP (100bb)
=====================================================
Positions: Button = Small Blind (SB). Acts FIRST preflop, LAST postflop. Other player = Big Blind (BB).

2A. BUTTON OPEN
- Open 80-100% of hands. Only the worst offsuit trash (72o, 82o, 83o, 92o, 93o, 73o) is borderline/mixed.
- Raise size: 2-2.5bb.
- Limping is a valid mixed option with medium/weak hands that play well postflop (suited connectors, small pairs, weak suited aces). Raising everything is simpler and strong.

2B. BB DEFENSE vs 2.5bb open
- Defend 60-70% of hands. Needs ~28-30% equity.
- CALL: all pairs not 3-bet, all suited hands, connected hands, most aces, most kings, many queens/jacks.
- FOLD: worst offsuit hands (J2o-, T3o-, 94o-, 85o-, 74o-, etc.).
- 3-BET 12-18%, split into:
  VALUE: AA, KK, QQ, JJ, TT, 99, AKs, AKo, AQs, AQo, some AJs, KQs.
  BLUFFS: A5s-A2s, K5s-K2s, some suited connectors (76s, 65s), Q9s/J9s type hands. Prefer blockers to calling range with decent equity when called.
- 3-bet size: 7-10bb OOP (about 3.5-4x the open).

2C. BUTTON vs 3-BET
- Continue 50-65% of opening range.
- 4-BET 8-12%: value AA, KK, QQ, AKs, AKo. Bluffs A5s-A2s, some suited broadways.
- CALL: JJ-66, AQ, AJs, KQs, suited broadways, suited connectors, pairs with position.
- FOLD: weakest opens.
- 4-bet size: 20-22bb.
- Against frequent 3-bettors: widen 4-bet bluffs and calls.

2D. BB vs 4-BET
- Jam/call with top of range (AA, KK, QQ, AK). Fold most bluffs unless opponent over-4-bets.
- 5-bet all-in with AA-QQ, AK, plus a few blocker bluffs (A5s).

=====================================================
3. SHORTER STACKS
=====================================================
20-40bb:
- Smaller opens (2bb or less), smaller 3-bets, many hands become 3-bet-or-fold instead of call.
- Low SPR, so jams common, overbets rare.

10-20bb:
- SB mixes raise-fold and jam. Limping gets worse (BB can jam over it).
- BB defends wide vs small raises, shoves over limps with a polar range.

Under 10bb (PUSH/FOLD):
- SB jams ~65-100% of hands depending on depth.
- BB calls ~50-65% at 8bb. Use exact Nash push/fold charts by stack size.

=====================================================
4. RANGE vs NUT ADVANTAGE (decides who bets and how big)
=====================================================
Range advantage (higher average equity): bet OFTEN and SMALL.
Nut advantage (more of the very best hands): bet BIG and polarized.
Capped range (no strongest hands): check or bet small, avoid big bets.

Preflop raiser usually has the advantage on high-card boards (A-high, K-high, Q-high).
Defender has more of the advantage on low connected boards (7-6-5, 8-7-4, 6-5-3).

=====================================================
5. FLOP PLAN BY TEXTURE
=====================================================
Dry high (A-7-2r, K-8-3r, Q-6-2r):
  Aggressor bets ~85-100% of range, SMALL (25-33% pot).
Medium (J-8-4 two-tone, Q-9-5 two-tone, K-T-4):
  Mixed bet/check, sizes 33-66%, check some strong hands to protect check range.
Very wet (9-8-7 two-tone, T-9-6 two-tone, J-T-9):
  Aggressor checks more, bets larger (66-100%) with polarized range.
Low / middle (7-4-2, 6-5-3):
  Defender's range is strong. Aggressor mixes many checks.
Paired (K-K-4, 7-7-2, A-A-6):
  Aggressor bets small (25-33%) with a wide range.
Monotone (all one suit):
  Small bets, high frequency for whoever holds the top flush cards. Lots of checks otherwise.
Ace-high boards: huge c-bet for the raiser. King/queen-high: still strong.

=====================================================
6. BY SITUATION
=====================================================
6A. Button, preflop raiser, in position (single-raised pot)
- C-bet 60-85% (higher on dry boards).
- Small size on boards that favor you, large when you have nut advantage.
- Bet: value + bluffs + weak showdown hands that need protection.
- Check back: medium-strength hands with showdown value, plus some strong hands to protect the check range.

6B. BB 3-bettor, out of position
- C-bet less. Mostly small bets or checks.
- Keep bluffs that block calls and have backdoor draws.

6C. Button defending vs BB 3-bet/raise, in position
- Call wide. Raise some draws and strong hands.
- Float with backdoor equity and bet when checked to later.

6D. BB defending out of position
- CHECK-RAISE 8-14% on flop: strong made hands + strong draws (flush draws, open-enders, combo draws).
- CHECK-CALL: medium made hands and weaker draws.
- DONK BET rarely, but fine on boards where you have nut advantage (low/middle boards).
- Check-raise size ~3x the bet (about 3.5x out of position).

6E. 3-bet pots (SPR ~4-5)
- Top pair is often a stack-off hand.
- Aggressor c-bets small and often.

6F. 4-bet pots (SPR ~1-2)
- Money usually goes in on flop with decent hands. Plan stack-offs in advance.

=====================================================
7. TURN
=====================================================
- Ranges become polarized. Bet larger (66-100%) with strong value and good bluffs. Check back marginal hands.
- DOUBLE BARREL with: draws, overcards with backdoor flush, and on cards that improve YOUR range more than theirs.
- CHECK BACK blanks that help the defender.
- Defend near MDF, but fold bottom-of-range hands with no equity.
- PROBE (BB bets into button who checked flop) with a polarized range, especially after cards that help you.
- DELAYED C-BET: check flop in position, bet turn. Opponent's checking range is capped.

=====================================================
8. RIVER
=====================================================
- Ranges are polarized: nuts, strong value, or bluffs.
- VALUE BET THIN. Heads-up ranges are wide, so second pair and top pair weak kicker often have value.
- BLUFF with blockers: hold cards that block the opponent's calling hands and unblock their folding hands (example: ace of a flush suit when flush is possible).
- BLUFF-CATCH with hands that beat bluffs and lose to value. Use MDF as the baseline. Call MORE vs aggressive opponents.
- SIZING: big (75-150% pot, even overbets) with nut advantage. Small when targeting thin calls.
- Don't bluff calling stations. Don't call tight players who only bet the nuts.
- Bluff-to-value ratios: use the table in section 1.

=====================================================
9. SPECIAL TACTICS
=====================================================
Check-raising: do it with BOTH bluffs and value or your check range is weak and readable. Best on boards where the aggressor's range is weak or who bets too many weak hands.
Overbetting: with a big nut advantage when the opponent has many bluff-catchers. 150-200% pot needs ~40-45% bluffs to be balanced.
Slowplaying: rare. Mostly on very dry boards (K-7-2r) so your checking range contains strong hands. Almost never on wet boards (free cards).
Floating: call a bet planning to bet later. Best in position vs players who give up on later streets.
Blind stealing: you post a blind every hand, so unopposed pots are worth a lot. Apply constant pressure.

=====================================================
10. EXPLOIT TABLE
=====================================================
Opponent tendency               -> Exploit
Folds too much preflop          -> Raise more, 3-bet bluff more, steal relentlessly
Calls too much (station)        -> Stop bluffing, value bet thinner and bigger, raise for value
Over-aggressive                 -> Call down lighter, check-raise more, trap
Too passive                     -> Bluff when they check, fold to their big bets
Folds to c-bets too often       -> C-bet 100% of range
Never folds to c-bets           -> C-bet only for value or with equity
Doesn't bluff river             -> Fold bluff-catchers to river bets
Bluffs river too much           -> Call wide with any bluff-catcher
Over-folds to 3-bets            -> 3-bet very wide
Never 4-bets                    -> 3-bet for value more, fold less to nothing
Sizing tells (small=weak, big=strong) -> Fold/call accordingly
Min-raises everything           -> Call wide, mix in 3-bets

Exploitation rules:
- Adjust gradually. Deviate more as the sample grows.
- Track: fold-to-3-bet, c-bet %, fold-to-c-bet, river raise %, etc.
- Small samples lie. Don't swing hard after 20 hands.
- Beware opponents who change gears. Keep the baseline sound.

=====================================================
11. BEATING OTHER BOTS
=====================================================
Typical bot weaknesses:
- Fixed bet sizes -> map each size to a hand strength.
- Bad handling of off-tree sizes -> try 35% pot, 1.7x pot, 3.1x raise. They round to the nearest known size and make mistakes.
- No randomization -> same hand always does the same thing, so pattern is readable.
- Threshold rules ("raise if equity > X") -> bet when they are just below the threshold.
- Folds too much or calls too much to all-ins.
- One stack depth only -> play at other depths.

Vs a strong equilibrium bot:
- You cannot reliably exploit it. Best result is break-even.
- Edge only comes from finer bet sizes, better card abstraction, and real-time re-solving.

=====================================================
12. META-STRATEGY FOR A BOT THAT DOESN'T LOSE
=====================================================
1. Unexploitable core: correct MDF, correct bluff ratios, balanced ranges by street, mixed actions.
2. Cover bet sizes: small, medium, large, overbet, all-in. Map odd opponent sizes to the nearest ones.
3. Button far more aggressive than BB.
4. Separate strategies for 100bb, 40bb, 20bb, and push/fold.
5. Exploit only with strong evidence and limited deviation.
6. Randomize so hand strength is never revealed by a pattern.
7. Test vs: very aggressive, very passive, tight, loose, and equilibrium-style opponents.

=====================================================
13. COMMON MISTAKES
=====================================================
- Playing too tight preflop
- Giving up after one called c-bet
- Never check-raising
- Over-bluffing players who rarely fold
- Paying off rivers vs passive players who only bet the nuts
- Ignoring stack depth
- Sizing tells (big with strong, small with weak)
- Using one bet size for everything
- Folding too much vs aggression (ignoring MDF)
- Treating short results as proof (variance is huge)

=====================================================
14. QUICK CHEAT SHEET
=====================================================
Button: raise 85-100%, c-bet 65-80%, constant pressure.
BB: defend 60-70% vs small raise, 3-bet 12-18% polarized, check-raise 8-14%.
Sizes: small (25-40%) when range advantage, large (66-150%) when polarized.
Defense: MDF = pot/(pot+bet). Bluffs = bet/(pot+2*bet).
River: thin value, blocker bluffs, bluff-catch vs aggression.
Short stacks: push/fold charts under ~10bb.
Exploit: more bluffs vs folders, more value vs callers, call down vs aggressors, fold to passive players' big bets.
